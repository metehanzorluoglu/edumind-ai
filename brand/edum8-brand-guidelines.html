01 Brand Philosophy

The mark is a magnifying glass, not a mascot.

EduM8 says: look closer, and you'll find the answer. Every part of the system below exists to protect that one idea — search, research, retrieval, evidence — rather than to decorate it. If a design choice doesn't serve legibility or that meaning, it doesn't belong in this system.

ORANGE "8"

TWO COLORS ONLY

The bar we're designing to: ChatGPT's sunburst, Claude's asterisk, Perplexity's compass, Linear's spiral. One shape. Flat color. No ornament.

2.3 — Construction

  • The lens is a perfect circle; the handle extends from the bottom-right at ~45°, capped to match the ring's stroke weight.
  • Stroke weight is constant across ring and handle — no taper, no line-weight contrast.
  • The "8" sits at the lens's optical center, not its mathematical center — its top counter is slightly smaller, so true visual balance sits just above dead-center.
  • Two colors, ever: brand blue for the glass, brand orange for the "8." No third color, no outline, no shadow.

2.4 — Clear space

Minimum clear space on every side equals the diameter of the lens opening — the inner circle the "8" sits inside. Call that unit 1×. Nothing crosses into it: text, UI chrome, the edge of the viewport.

Why two oranges? Amber only reaches 2.3:1 contrast on white — fine for a large graphic mark, not for text. Rather than dull the logo into a WCAG-safe brown, amber stays in the mark, and the product's existing ochre carries orange wherever it needs to function as text or a UI signal. 05 Icon System

Two constructions: one for tiny sizes, one for everything else.

5.1 — Favicon specification

Problem found in production: the primary mark's "8" is a serif numeral with thin strokes and tight counters. Rendered at 16×16 and 32×32, it visibly collapses. Here's the actual test, at real pixel size:

The fix: rebuild the "8" from two stacked circular rings instead of the typographic numeral, and thicken both the ring and the "8" strokes. Still "blue magnifying glass, orange 8, nothing else" — the two-circle construction happens to reinforce the "continuous loop / infinite learning" meaning even more directly than the typographic version. Use this single construction across the whole favicon family (16–64px), not just the smallest size.

5.2 — App icon specification

White background, the detailed symbol (the typographic "8" is fine at these sizes) centered with generous margin — the mark occupies ~68% of canvas width, matching iOS's safe-zone convention so OS masking never crops it. No pre-applied rounded-rect or circle mask — every platform applies its own at install time.

06 Spacing Rules

The lens sets the unit.

  • Clear space: 1× on all sides, where 1× = the lens opening diameter (§2.4).
  • Symbol-to-wordmark gap in the primary lockup: fixed at 0.35× the lens diameter, scaling with the lockup — never stretched or compressed independently.
  • Grid alignment: when placing the symbol in UI chrome, align its optical center, not its bounding-box center — the handle's weight in the bottom-right makes true bounding-box centering read as slightly off.

08 Accessibility

Measured, not assumed.

Ochre #B0641F on white4.5:1AA text

Rules of thumb

  • The logo itself is exempt from text-contrast rules (standard WCAG logo exception) — ship it in full brand color everywhere, per §2.
  • The moment orange carries text or a functional signal — not just decorating the mark — switch from amber to ochre.
  • Blue on dark ink (3.3:1) is fine for the logo and large UI graphics; for small body text on dark surfaces, use a lighter blue tint instead (this page's own dark-mode links do exactly that).
  • Never rely on color alone to convey meaning — e.g. "orange = AI-generated" needs an icon or label too, for colorblind readers.
